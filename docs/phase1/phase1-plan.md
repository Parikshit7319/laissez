# Phase 1 plan: customer discovery, 12 weeks

Start Monday 5 October 2026. Week 12 ends Sunday 27 December 2026. Weeks 10 to 12 overlap exams and holidays, so the interviews are front-loaded into weeks 2 to 9 and the last three weeks are for the issuer and counsel threads, which move on their own clock anyway.

Time budget: 8 to 10 hours a week alongside coursework. Calls with Singapore and Hong Kong happen between 7 pm and 10 pm Houston time, which is 8 am to 11 am the next day there. Block Tuesday and Thursday evenings.

## The goal

By week 12, know whether distributors feel this problem badly enough to pay for it, whether issuers will let a third party issue claims on their registry, and roughly what counsel will cost before anything touches real money. Then decide: build toward a paid pilot, change the wedge, or stop.

## Go or no-go at week 12

All five, or it is a no-go and the question becomes what to change:

1. At least 10 discovery interviews completed with the people in `target-list.md` groups 1 to 3 (distributors), notes filled and scored.
2. At least 3 of them describe cross-border tokenized fund onboarding as a top-three problem for their desk, unprompted or in answer to "where does this rank".
3. At least 1 signed design partner letter of intent on the terms in `design-partner-terms.md` (a one-paragraph email from a sponsor counts if it names the corridor and the two people).
4. At least 1 issuer or transfer agent yes-in-principle to registering Laissez as a trusted claim issuer for one fund on testnet, with the condition they set written down.
5. A scoping estimate in hand from at least one law firm in Singapore and one in Hong Kong for the three questions below.

Partial results are information too. Two of five with strong signal on pain and no issuer yes means the wedge is distributor-only for now. Five of five with weak pain scores means the problem is real but not urgent, and the pricing hypothesis needs to drop.

## Counsel scoping

Three questions to put to a Singapore firm and a Hong Kong firm. The point is a scoping estimate (hours and a range), not an opinion yet.

1. **Perimeter.** If Laissez issues eligibility credentials, evaluates rules and instructs delivery-versus-payment settlement of tokenized fund units and stablecoins, but never custodies assets, never executes as principal and never gives advice, which licensable activities, if any, does that touch in your jurisdiction (in Singapore under the SFA and PSA, in Hong Kong under the SFO and the stablecoin and VATP regimes), and what structure keeps Laissez outside the perimeter or inside it with the lightest licence?
2. **Reliance.** Under what conditions can a licensed distributor in your jurisdiction rely on an investor classification and KYC performed by another regulated firm, delivered as a signed credential with citation and evidence reference, and what must the reliance agreement and the credential contain for the relying firm's compliance to accept it?
3. **Rule packs.** What would it take for your firm to review and sign off the rule pack for your jurisdiction (accredited and professional investor tests, restricted offer conditions, document requirements, Travel Rule thresholds) as a condition of production access, how often would it need re-review, and what is the estimate for the first review?

Ask each firm for: the partner who would own it, the estimated hours for a scoping memo on the three questions, the hourly range, and whether they do fixed-fee scoping for early-stage companies.
